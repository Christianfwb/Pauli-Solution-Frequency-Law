# Act IV — What Changes
## The Ordering, the Gaps, and What Would Settle It

---

## Where This Act Sits

Acts I through III established a reading, not a result.

Pauli's exclusion principle, Schrödinger's Zitterbewegung, the 4π structure of
spinors — all of it stands in standard physics without this framework. What the
Frequency Law added was a proposed direction of explanation, and Act III was
explicit that the bridge it needs has not been derived.

By the standard set in the Prologue:

> **If changing the direction of reading changes nothing observable, it is ontology.**
> **If it produces a novel quantitative prediction that survives experiment, it becomes physics.**

Acts I–III are ontology.

This act is where the framework attempts the second thing.

## The Ordering

If frequency is taken as primary, particles order naturally by Compton frequency
rather than by mass, charge, or generation.

The ordering itself contains nothing new. `f = mc²/h` applied to known masses
reproduces known masses — a consistency check with no evidential weight, as
stated throughout.

What the ordering does is make **gaps** visible.

Mendeleev's ordering by atomic mass had gaps too, and three of them were later
filled. That precedent is encouraging and proves nothing. Gaps in an ordering
are only interesting if something is actually found in them.

## Two Candidate Windows

| | Berrangium Ω | Stöcker Σ |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass** | ~16.2 MeV/c² | ~530 MeV/c² |
| **f_compton** | ~3.9 × 10²¹ Hz | ~1.3 × 10²³ Hz |
| **Position** | between electron and muon | between muon and proton |
| **Nearby anomaly** | ~17 MeV signal reported by the Atomki group in several nuclear transitions; interpretation disputed, awaiting decisive independent confirmation | f₀(500) resonance — broad, long-known, persistently poor fit to the quark model |

Both values sit near existing experimental puzzles, and were identified with
those puzzles in view. That is stated plainly because it matters: a number
chosen with knowledge of where an anomaly sits is not the same thing as a number
derived in ignorance of it.

Until the second kind exists, these are **candidate windows** — assignments of
open questions to gaps in an ordering.

## On the Derivation

The route from the axioms to these two values is not published here.

That is a decision, and it should be named as one rather than left as a silence.
This release contains the ordering, the candidate values, and the framework that
motivates them. It does not contain the derivation.

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions.

**What it costs.** A closed derivation cannot be audited. No reader can check
whether the values follow from the axioms or were fitted to the anomalies. That
is a real and permanent limitation of this release, and no amount of framing
removes it.

**What it does not cost.** Falsifiability. A prediction can be tested without
access to the reasoning that produced it. A useful analogy is a compiler: it can
be evaluated as a black box by fixing an input, recording the expected output in
advance, and comparing that output against an independent result. Nobody needs
the source to run that test.

The analogy has limits — a physical framework is not a piece of software, and
the comparison is offered for the testing procedure only, not as a claim about
what the framework is.

> **A closed derivation limits auditability; it does not remove falsifiability.**
> **The source can remain closed. The predictions cannot.**

The second sentence is the binding one. A value published without a tolerance
band and without a stated failure condition is not a prediction — it is a
gesture toward an anomaly, and it can never be wrong. If the derivation stays
closed, the *output* has to be that much more explicit.

## What Would Count, and What Would Not

**Would not count:**

- A resonance appearing anywhere in the general region. Without a tolerance
  band fixed in advance, any result is compatible with the claim.
- Identifying X17 with Berrangium Ω after the fact. The value was chosen with
  X17 in view; agreement is therefore expected and carries no information.
- Reproducing known masses from Compton frequencies. That returns what was
  put in.

**Would count:**

- A stated value with a stated tolerance, published before the relevant
  measurement, that survives it.
- A signature — width, spin/parity, decay pattern — that standard models do not
  predict and that is subsequently observed.
- A stated result that would falsify the claim, followed by an experiment that
  does not produce it.

That is the whole test. It is not a high bar in principle. It is simply a bar
that has not yet been cleared.

## Where This Leaves the Framework

Honestly stated:

**What exists.** A consistent re-reading of standard relations. An ordering
principle that produces visible gaps. Two candidate windows near two genuine
open questions in experimental physics.

**What does not exist yet.** A published derivation. Tolerance bands. Signatures.
Exclusion criteria. And — from Act III — the bridge explaining why a geometric
factor of two and a dynamical factor of two, which run in opposite directions,
should share a cause.

**What that adds up to.** An ontology with a candidate route toward physics,
which has not yet been taken.

The Prologue said that if the choice about time is wrong, everything after it
fails. That remains true. What Act IV adds is that even if the choice is right,
the framework does not yet demonstrate it.

The candidate windows are either supported by independently specified signatures
or they are not. A resonance sitting somewhere in the vicinity is not enough —
as the f₀(500) case makes plain, a region can already be occupied without that
occupancy confirming anything.

That question is not settled by argument, and it is not settled by this document.
